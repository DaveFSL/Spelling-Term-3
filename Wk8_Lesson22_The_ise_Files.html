The Briefing

The showdown that starts the case — plus a quick retrieval check on last week's theme words.

1. The showdown: which spelling belongs in Australian writing?

Fix-up: In Australia we write organise with an s. The z version isn't wrong everywhere — it's standard in America — but it's the wrong passport for Australian writing.

2. Retrieval from Week 7: which is the correct spelling?

Fix-up: sanc-tu-a-ry — the hidden a before -ry. Robot-talk it if your ears try to skip it.

3. Which word is the rebel that takes -yse instead of -ise?

Fix-up: Analyse is the rebel — it comes from a different Greek family (the same one as paralyse), so it keeps its y. Recognise and summarise are regular -ise citizens.

4. What does summarise mean?

Fix-up: To summarise is to shrink something to its main ideas — a summary. Copying word-for-word isn't summarising, and adding numbers is summing, a different word.

5. Why do these verbs trip spellers up? When you say organise, the -ise ending sounds like…

Fix-up: The s in -ise sounds like /z/ — organize is exactly what your ears hear. That's why you can't trust them here: this is an eyes-and-rules job, not a sounding-out job.

02

Case Notes

Four cards: the rule, where it came from, the rebels, and how to handle American imports.

The rule: Australia writes -ise

Verbs that mean "to make or to do something" — organise, recognise, summarise, hypothesise — take -ise in Australian English. It's a generalisation: a rule that works across thousands of words, in every subject you study.

Remember: If you're writing in Australia, the ending is -ise. Every subject, every time.

Why two spellings exist

Both endings grew from the same ancient Greek verb ending. Centuries later, English split: American dictionaries standardised -ize, while British English — which Australian English follows — settled on -ise. Neither is "broken"; they're two branches of one family tree. Your job is to write on the Australian branch.

Remember: -ise and -ize are cousins. Australia sides with the British cousin.

The -yse rebels

A small gang of words never joined the -ise family: analyse (and its cousin paralyse) come from the Greek word lysis, meaning "loosening apart". They keep their y: -yse. Writing "analise" or "analize" gets stopped at the border either way.

Remember: analyse — when you analyse, you loosen a problem apart. Keep the y.

Reading vs writing

You will meet -ize in the wild — American books, websites and games use it constantly, and it's correct there. A good word detective does two different jobs: recognise -ize when reading (don't panic, it's the same word) and choose -ise when writing.

Remember: Read both. Write -ise.
