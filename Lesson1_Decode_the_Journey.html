Case Notes

Four facts every word detective keeps in their notebook. Come back to these any time you get stuck.

1 · A base word + a suffix

A base word is the core word we build from. A suffix is a word-part we add to the end to make a new word.

Remember: Find the base word first — the rest of the spelling falls into place.

2 · “-ous” means “full of”

Adding -ous turns a noun into an adjective that means “full of” or “having” that thing.

Remember: dangerous = full of danger · courageous = having courage.

3 · Soft “g” keeps its “e”

If we dropped the “e”, the “g” in courag-ous would go hard. The “e” protects the soft sound.

Remember: Keep the “e” after a soft c or g when the suffix starts with a, o or u.

4 · The suffix family

Different suffixes carry different meanings — and some change the base spelling: survive drops its “e” before -al.

Remember: -dom = a state · -al = the act of · -ee = a person who.

Crack the Case

Put the words to work. Write 2–3 sentences about a refugee’s journey — you might picture Jamal from Boy Overboard. Use at least three focus words and underline each one.

Focus words: refugee · asylum · journey · survival · freedom · desperate · dangerous · courageous

Sentence starters

• The refugee family began a …
• It was a dangerous …
• They were desperate to reach …
• More than anything, they hoped for freedom and …

Cloze — fill the gaps:

The ______ family began a dangerous ______ across the sea. They were ______ but ______, hoping to find ______ in a safer country.

Tap a word to drop it into your writing box.

🔒 Write at least one sentence of your own first — then the worked example unlocks.
Worked example (one way to do it):
Desperate for safety, the refugee family began a dangerous journey across the open sea. Their survival depended on staying brave, and they held onto the hope of freedom in a new country. That uses six focus words. Yours only needs three — check each is spelt correctly using your Case Notes.
