02

Case Notes

Four facts every data detective keeps in their notebook. Come back to these any time you get stuck on a long word.

1 · Chunk it into beats

Break a long word into syllables (beats). Clap or tap each beat, then spell the chunks one by one — much safer than guessing the whole word.

Remember: Chop it into beats, spell each beat, then join them back up.

2 · “-tion” = an action or process

The ending -tion turns a word into the action or process of doing it. It is one beat and sounds like /shun/.

Remember: -tion = a process (estimation, rotation) — always spelt t-i-o-n.

3 · “-ity” = a state or quality

The ending -ity makes a noun meaning a state or quality. Listen for the “it-ee” at the end and spell it i-t-y.

Remember: -ity = a quality (probability, ability, activity).

4 · “-ical” = to do with

The ending -ical means “relating to” or “to do with”. It is spelt i-c-a-l — watch you don’t write “-icle”.

Remember: -ical = to do with (categorical, statistical, logical).

04

Ending Detective

Three endings do most of the heavy lifting in data words. Tap one to reveal what it means, how it’s spelt, and which words use it.

-tion → an action or process. One beat, said /shun/, always spelt t-i-o-n.

In our words: estimation (the act of estimating). You’ll meet it everywhere: rotation, information, population.

-ity → a state or quality. Listen for “it-ee” and spell it i-t-y.

In our words: probability (how likely something is). Also: ability, activity, quantity.

-ical → relating to / to do with. Spelt i-c-a-l — not “-icle”.

In our words: categorical (data sorted into groups). Also: statistical, logical, numerical.

06

Crack the Case

Put the words to work. Write 2–3 sentences describing a graph or survey result. Use at least three focus words and underline each one.

Focus words: statistics · frequency · percentage · average · estimation · variable · categorical · probability

Sentence starters

• Our frequency table showed that …
• The average result was …
• A large percentage of the class …
• Favourite sport is categorical data because …

Cloze — fill the gaps:

We used ______ to study our survey. The ______ table showed the most common answer, and the ______ score was 7. Favourite colour is ______ data because it sorts into groups.

Tap a word to drop it into your writing box.

🔒 Write at least one sentence of your own first — then the worked example unlocks.
Worked example (one way to do it):
We used statistics to make sense of our survey. The frequency table showed the most common shoe size, and the average was size 6. Favourite sport is categorical data, so the probability of picking a soccer fan was high. That uses five focus words. Yours only needs three — check each is spelt correctly using your Case Notes.
