Case Notes

Four facts about the word-parts that build theme vocabulary. Suffixes change a word’s job; prefixes change its meaning.

1 · -ment makes a noun

-ment turns a verb into a noun naming the action or result: displace → displacement.

Remember: -ment = the result of doing something.

2 · -ence & -ity name a quality

-ence and -ity build nouns that name a quality or state: resilience, identity.

Remember: -ence / -ity = a quality you can’t touch.

3 · Prefixes dis- & im-

dis- means away/apart (displacement). im- means into or not (immigrant, impossible).

Remember: prefixes flip or shift the meaning.

4 · -ion turns verbs into nouns

-ion makes a noun from a verb: persecute → persecution. It’s the one light sound clue this lesson — say /shun/.

Remember: -ion = an action turned into a thing.

Theme Writing

Use the words for real. In 2–3 sentences, write about how a character might show courage or find belonging on a hard journey. Use at least three focus words and underline each.

Focus words: courage · resilience · displacement · belonging · identity · compassion · persecution · sanctuary

Sentence starters

• After the displacement of her family, she showed …
• His resilience helped him keep hoping for belonging …
• The strangers treated them with compassion when …
• Even after persecution, they held on to their identity …

Cloze — fill the gaps:

It took real ______ to leave home. After months of ______, the family longed for ______ and a safe ______ where they could be themselves.
