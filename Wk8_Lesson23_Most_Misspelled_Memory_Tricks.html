The Briefing

The class-vote classics: five spellings adults get wrong all the time. Trust your eyes, not your ears.

1. Which is correct?

Fix-up: sep-a-rate — the middle vowel is an a, not an e. The trick you'll learn today: there's a rat hiding in separate.

2. Which is correct?

Fix-up: def-i-nite-ly. The mumbled vowel is an i — and the whole word finite is hiding inside: definitely.

3. Which is correct?

Fix-up: One c, two s's — necessary. Today's trick: a shirt has one collar and two sleeves.

4. Retrieval from last lesson: which spelling clears Australian customs?

Fix-up: recognise — Australian English takes -ise. The z is the American import.

5. Why do words like separate and definitely trap so many spellers?

Fix-up: It's the mumbling-vowel problem (the schwa) from Week 7: say sep'rate fast and the middle vowel vanishes. That's why we build memory tricks — they tell your hand what your ears can't.

Case Notes

The Mnemonic Gallery: one trick per trap word, plus why tricks work at all.

separate: there's a rat in it

The vowel everyone gets wrong is the middle a — because your voice mumbles it. So catch the stowaway: sepARATe has a rat sitting right in the middle. Spot the rat, and you'll never write "seperate" again.

Remember: There's a rat in separate.

definitely: finite inside

People write "definately" because the middle vowel mumbles. But this word is built from finite (meaning limited): de + finite + ly. If you can spell finite, the middle of definitely is done — no a anywhere.

Remember: deFINITEly — spell the finite, and the word spells itself.

necessary: one collar, two sleeves

How many c's? How many s's? Picture a shirt: it is necessary to wear one — and a shirt has one collar (one c) and two sleeves (two s's). ne-c-e-ss-ary.

Remember: A shirt is necessary: 1 collar (c), 2 sleeves (ss).

Why memory tricks work

Your brain forgets plain letter strings, but it's brilliant at pictures and little stories. A mnemonic converts the forgettable bit (which vowel? how many s's?) into something vivid — a rat, a shirt, a hidden word. The sillier the picture, the stickier it is.

Remember: Can't hear it? Make a picture for it. Silly = sticky.
